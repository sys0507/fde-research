# Stage 1: Decomposing the Vague Request and Preparing Interviews

## 1. Original mandate and business scope

- **Original mandate**: [The exact wording, the requester's role, and the triggering background.]
- **Business outcome they want improved**: [What the requester wants to change, distinguishing the business goal from the technical approach they imagine.]
- **Known facts and undetermined items**: [Facts with their sources; list "possible causes" separately as pending verification.]
- **Investigation boundary**: [From which business event to which result, covering which objects and periods.]

## 2. Preliminary business chain

Describe the currently known main path in terms of role actions; for each step note the responsible role, input, system, output, and the condition for continuing. Names may be written where already known; leave uncertain role responsibilities as pending verification. This is the initial flow — later scenarios add branches onto this same flow.

## 3. Interviewees and the basis for selecting them

Examine stakeholders by "who set the goal, who does the actual work, who holds the facts, who has the authority to decide"; one role may cover several categories. State the basis on which each person was found — the organization introduction, an existing flow, or a material.

## 4. Whole business picture and investigation gaps

Use SIPOC to organize suppliers, inputs, process, outputs, and customers; point out which relationships are known and which remain unknown. You cannot declare that "unclear inputs cause the delay" straight from "the input is unclear."

| Element | Known content in this business and its basis | Information still needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Supplier S | [Who provides it] | [Pending verification] |
| Input I | [Material / condition] | [Pending verification] |
| Process P | [Key steps] | [Pending verification] |
| Output O | [Business result] | [Pending verification] |
| Customer C | [Who uses the result] | [Pending verification] |

## 5. Interview outline and collection plan

Derive each question from a gap above, making clear whom to ask, why, and what record you hope to see. Ask first about the most recent specific piece of business, then follow up on the current handling method, the actual burden, the basis for it, and a normal-case control. Schedule interviews, on-site observation, and material collection separately; they may be done in an interleaved way.

This stage produces the preliminary flow, the interview plan, and the collection tasks. The next stage reconstructs concrete business experiences from the actual interviews; without interview results, do not fill in problem conclusions ahead of time.
