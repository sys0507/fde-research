# Explain an existing implementation and quotation clearly

Use after estimation. Packages, people, dates, samples, terms, fees, and uncertainty come from the current model/analysis/originals. Writing explains decisions without redesigning them; more prose does not create more service. No word/page target applies.

## Give each section a customer purpose

Move naturally from conclusion to basis, arrangements, and conditions without forcing four subheadings. Scope explains deliverables; schedule explains order and timing constraints; acceptance explains receipt criteria; expenses explain payer, purpose, and omissions.

Start with this project's result/action/owner instead of generic “ensure quality” introductions. One paragraph handles one main point. Separate sample preparation, criteria, and failure treatment with descriptive subheadings rather than packing many arrangements together or using slogans.

## Explain tables

Before a table state purpose and shared basis, such as days counted after startup conditions. Afterward explain parallelism, real integration dependencies, and necessary windows without rereading every row. Put bulk detail in existing appendices, not extra binders/forms/approvals created for formality.

Reference existing numbers/dates. Separate active effort, construction duration, pilot observation, and later support. Rewording does not reschedule. Thousands separators and units may improve display while preserving value/precision/basis. Retain proposed, unquoted, and confirmed qualifications.

## Explain technical roles without changing scope

Keep names needed for decisions and explain their project purpose on first use; neither internal shorthand nor a technical textbook helps the customer. For example, “On resume, recheck the operator's access and source version so revoked authority or stale material is not reused” explains an existing recovery rule without adding one. “Show payment plans separately from actual payment status” does not guarantee settlement.

Do not expose person IDs, JSON fields, script statuses, or internal modeling debates in customer prose. Put necessary detail in existing appropriate appendices and keep payroll/rates/margins internal.

## Responsibilities and conditions

Use concrete actors/actions, e.g. QA retains test records and the designated business owner confirms conclusions. Clarify existing processes without adding chairs, meetings, approval tiers, response promises, or free support.

Distinguish contracted service price, client-paid budget, and unquoted expenses; explain estimate versus settlement. Professional writing comes from clear responsibility/basis/conditions/results, not extra clauses. For wording-only work preserve accepted conclusions and record analytical doubts internally for authorized treatment.

## Small rewriting example

Source: “60 authorized sanitized samples: 30 development, 30 held out; normal/missing/corrected cases; replace contaminated holdouts.”

Expanded: “The proposal uses 60 authorized sanitized samples, with 30 for development and 30 for independent acceptance. They cover the already agreed normal, missing-material, and corrected-material situations rather than only familiar examples.

The designated QA and business roles control the holdout set. If a holdout informs error analysis or a system change, mark it as used for tuning and replace it with material not used during development before independent judgment.”

All numbers/actions come from the source. Reuse the writing method, not the quantities.

## Targeted final comparison

Check clarity of conclusions/reasons, retained conditions, and absence of unsourced numbers/scope/dates/commitments. Wording-only changes leave calculations intact. If a new Word is requested, use the established generation/rendering checks; otherwise call the output a draft or example, not a replaced final report.
