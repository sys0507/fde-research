## 1. The requester's original problem

Preserve the original wording, the role who raised it, the triggering situation, what they want changed, and the known constraints. Separately state the feelings, the facts, and the technical approach the requester imagined.

## 2. Business background and investigation scope

Start with a short paragraph on how this business runs and why it matters, then use one concrete case to help an unfamiliar reader understand the business object and the outcome. State the covered scope, the business not covered, the sampling, and the data period.

## 3. Current business flow and organization

State what each role is actually responsible for. Reconstruct the normal path and the representative branches: who uses which material or system, what judgment they make, and how it completes or goes back. Distinguish the policy, the system, and the actual operation, and keep the differences found during cross-checking.

## 4. Data, systems, and enterprise conditions

Show the system relationships, the main objects, an asset overview, and the meaning of fields and states. Describe data quality, update timing, read/write and access conditions, how usable the interfaces really are, and any environmental conditions that may affect the solution.

## 5. Business baseline and problem diagnosis

First summarize the identified problems in everyday business language. For each problem, state how it is handled today, the extra labor or waiting it causes, the cause supported by evidence, and the impact. For existing metrics, give the sample, unit, denominator, time range, source, and calculation method; state verbal estimates, simulations, and unmeasured items separately.

## 6. AI opportunities and value assessment

Starting from the causes, compare conventional process/rule improvement against the AI increment. Form a combination suited to this business around information foundation building, decision support, and bounded automation. For each item, state which work it improves, the basis for its value, the conditions required, the result that can be verified, and the current limits; benefits without evidence must not be written as commitments.

## 7. Recommended priority areas and verification focus

Give this round's recommendation and the reasoning directly: what to improve first, what not to choose for now, how far the current facts can support it, and which key unknowns would affect the choice. State which business results or operational burdens will be used to judge whether to continue. Keep the depth at the opportunity-recommendation level; the detailed technical design and development plan are produced separately.
